I noted in
[Variation and Change]({{'/2026/10/03/variation-and-change'|relative_url}})
that I began a job search in early 2026.
LinkedIn seemed a natural place to start, as I have a fairly deep
network of contacts from the API industry, and what I think is a strong
background.

I quickly found out a few things about searching for jobs on LinkedIn,
and decided to share.

![Banner graphic with a dark blue/grey geometic background, with white text on a blue background reading "What's With LinkedIn Requiting?", and a block reading "API Design Matters" and a thurd block on end reading "David J Biesack]({{

LinkedIn (even with Premium access) does not do a very good job at
search.

I conducted *dozens* of searches for API jobs, and invariably,
LinkedIn found tons of AI jobs instead. For example, if I searched
for "Vice President of APIs" or "Director of APIs", I would find no direct matches,
but dozens of close matches for "VP of AI". Thanks, but no,
matching "AI" instead of "API" is **not** helpful.

Secondly, I saw that AI was sucking up **all the air** in the etherwebs. So, so many
jobs for _VP of AI_, _Head of AI_, _Head of AI Strategy_, _VP AI Governance_....
Have companies really gone all in on AI to that degree, completely ignoring
the core technology upon which all those AI integrations depend,
hiring out of FOMO, or because of AI vendor hype?
Sigh.

The third thing I soon discovered was the large number of *scam recruiters*
out there. I had quite a number of them who start out with flattery
("You have a very strong background that meets our needs..."), but when I
learn more, it seemed they are really trying to scam people through
identity theft and [pig butchering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_butchering_scam).

Way too many.

[Tip: if they say their hiring manager will contact you via Signal
or WhatsApp -- drop them immediately. There is lots of evidence
(here, or Reddit, or other forums) that this is an immediate sign
of trouble. Heed that advice. It's true. Sad, but true.]

There seems to be a pattern of whipping up a job description based
on your LinkedIn profile -- one that looks like a great match, doing the
type of work that has excited me for a dozen years -- really niche work
(after all, there were never too many people with jobs like "Chief API
Officer").

Seems like using ChatGPT or Claude to create a compelling job
description is just way too easy, so if it looks too good to be true, it
likely is.

Of course, if the "recruiter" does not have an established profile on
LinkeIn (I've seen many who are students or work in unrelated fields,
often with `#OpenToWork` tags on their LinkedIn profiles) is
another red flag. (It's possible/probable they are hacked accounts.)

Wow, so close, you even used that really rare title. Is a company really
sourcing a C-level position through LinkedIn, or does it sound too good to be true?
Yup, that's very suspicious.

At the time, I was still very optimistic/hopeful (perhaps vulnerable...)
and I kind of fell for this
one to start, but after weeks, nothing panned out.... the "Board" approved my
application for a senior position, but the planned job fair
never materialized, the details were never provided, and when the
Operations Manager started asking personal questions and even shared
what they claimed to be a photo of themselves (which I assumed was
a fake AI image), I ceased communication. It became clear
this was a pig butchering attempt, or worse.

I have had at least **eight** such experiences, all initiated on LinkedIn.
I got very good at spotting the fraud, hoever.

By the time I got suspicious enough to report any of these,
the original contact on LinkedIn was no longer there...
no one to report. (One disappeared from LinkedIn within
hours of me declining to connect with the hiring manager
over Signal. So that clearly confirmed my suspicions. Perhaps
LinkedIn is actually intervening in these cases and shutting
down accounts??)

I still get these "opportunities" now, even though my LinkedIn
profile is no longer tagged "#OpenToWork". In fact, as I have been
drafting this post over the past week, I have had <strike>three</strike>
four such contacts.

The playbook is pretty similar -- eerily similar.

One wrote:

> Far Eastern Group has business interests across multiple sectors, and
> given your experience as a Technical Principal at Financial Data
> Exchange, areas such as financial data infrastructure, enterprise
> technology, data integration, and digital transformation may be
> relevant points to explore. However, the specific position details,
> responsibilities, and selection criteria still need to be confirmed,
> and I don't want to present unverified information as final. My
> employer, Zoe, is coordinating the recruitment process and would be
> the best person to clarify the available role details and discuss how
> they may align with your experience. If you're open to it, you can
> connect with her directly via WhatsApp or Signal for a more detailed
> conversation before deciding whether to proceed. Thank you again for
> your time and consideration.

and a _completely different_ person, allegedly "recruiting" for a _completely
different_ company, wrote:

> The formal job description and specific selection criteria are still
> being clarified, so I don't want to provide you with inaccurate or
> unverified details. Given your experience as a Technical Principal at
> Financial Data Exchange, your background may be relevant to
> opportunities involving enterprise technology, financial data
> infrastructure, data integration, or digital transformation, depending
> on the actual role requirements. My employer, Zoe, is coordinating the
> recruitment process and would be the best person to clarify the position
> scope and the client's expectations. If you're open to discussing it
> further, please connect with her directly via WhatsApp or Signal so you
> can review the details and determine whether the opportunity fits your
> expertise.

Clearly this is AI generated slop. They can't even take the time
to change the hiring manager's name... or to notice that my recent job tenure is
only 2 months, while ignoring my 38+ years of industry experience.

Perhaps it is a coincidence, but I think it is more the _modus operandi_:
for _every one_ of these, the hiring manger was (claimed to be) female
(Zoe or other .... )
I suspect the hucksters think most "victims" will trust a female more
than a male when buttering up a "candidate".

Oh, and don't forget about the people who start by contacting people as
"recruiters" but quickly push you on a resume writing service or their
own proprietary job board, or many other ways to separate you from your
money.... People can really demonstrate the worst in humanity when
taking advantage of vulnerable people. Sickening.

Months of watching LinkedIn's Recommended Jobs (most of which don't
match anyway... Why can't LinkedIn's algorithms _learn_ from all that data?!?), more
searches, etc. and I found only a few real opportunities.
(See above - I was not applying for typical tech jobs like software
engineer or software architect or engineering manager, where, even after all the hits
in the tech sector, there were a lot of open positions.)

Sadly, as so many have reported, even after writing cover letters and
updating my resume (highlighting my real experience - honest updates; I
never used AI to write these) to match the job posting, I was ghosted by
soooooo many companies... close to 70% never responded _at all_. These are not entry level
positions I was applying for, but high, senior/leadership level
positions, seeking people with many years of experience. But hey,
just a short email response telling me that you went with another
candidate is orders or magnitudes better than no followup at all. These
are real people you are dealing with - show a shred of decency and send
an email (and heck, if a few of the "applicants" really are just bots,
who cares if a bot gets an extra email, or your email bounces back...) Oh,
and don't wait 4 months to send a rejection. That's not respectful
either. I suspect many of the companies are rejecting applicants based
on AI Application Tracking Systems, but no one in HR wants that to come
to light, so ghosting is easier than writing a polite email.

(While you are looking for your next Head of AI, make sure they
know how to fix your sucky AI HR screening and sucky AI CRM.)

It must be incredibly frustrating right now to be a _legitimate
recruiter_, with all the hucksters defaming the profession
and tearing down any semblance of integrity, trust, and relationship
building which is core to good recruiting.

The risk of declining an opportunity from a "recruiter" on LinkedIn is real.
What if that one was really a legit recruiter who just happened to use
the same mothods as the scammers? I suppose this is why it's so easy to
scam someone who is out of a job and hopeful about any opportunity.

My conclusion is that recruiting on LinkedIn has been widely corrupted,
and I don't see LinkedIn doing anythnig to protet thier core business value.
